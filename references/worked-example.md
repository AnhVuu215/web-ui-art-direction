# Worked example: a travel homepage with a human point of view

This example demonstrates the skill's reasoning. It is not a starter template and contains no invented claims about a real business.

## Input brief

> “Make a Vietnamese travel homepage that feels adventurous and personal. Visitors should discover small-group trips and ask about one.”

Assumptions for a first pass: desktop and mobile web; trip inventory, prices, dates, and photos must come from the owner; visitors are comparing options rather than booking instantly.

## Two directions and a choice

1. **Field journal:** Warm off-white canvas, landscape photography, compact location notes, a readable editorial type system, and route-line details. This supports a slow, considered trip decision.
2. **Expedition control:** Dark interface, coordinates, technical labels, map-grid motifs, and animated route indicators. This feels more futuristic but competes with the warmth of small-group travel.

Choose **Field journal** for the stated audience and task. The visual idea is “a thoughtful guide's notebook.” The dominant move is authentic, place-specific photography. Supporting moves are editorial place names and small practical notes. Avoid decorative globes, fake stamps, unrelated 3D objects, and automatic full-screen slideshows.

## Direction translated into a page

| Moment | Content job | Visual and interaction decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Explain the trips and make the first action obvious | One real destination image with a stable dark text area; specific headline; “See upcoming trips” action leading to listings |
| Trip finder | Help visitors narrow choices | Location, duration, month, and budget only if those fields exist in real inventory; controls remain visible on mobile |
| Featured trips | Support comparison | Unequal card sizes for editorial rhythm, but consistent data positions for destination, dates, duration, price, and availability |
| Why this guide | Earn trust | A real guide portrait and a short account of how trips are chosen; no invented partner logos or review counts |
| Trip process | Reduce uncertainty | Three plain steps from enquiry to departure; FAQ and contact path nearby |
| Footer | Finish the journey | Contact, policies, and links with normal legible type |

## System decisions

- **Type:** A characterful display face for destinations, a clear sans-serif for navigation, forms, pricing, and details. Do not use tiny all-caps body copy.
- **Color:** Paper-like canvas, dark ink, one earth-toned accent. Action color must stay clear against photography and white surfaces.
- **Layout:** Narrower text measure than image width; photos can reach the page edge while comparison data follows one alignment grid.
- **Image direction:** Consistent natural light, human-scale viewpoints, real locations. Crop to preserve subjects and provide separate mobile crops if needed.
- **Details:** Short route line may connect sections; date chips and captions share one border and radius family. No motif should interfere with a price or action.
- **Motion:** Restrained card transitions that explain state. Reduced-motion users get the same content and controls.

## Review before hand-off

Check the first viewport at desktop and mobile sizes; keyboard through filters/cards; long Vietnamese place names; missing price/date; empty search results; enquiry errors; 200% zoom; contrast on every image crop; and image loading/layout stability. If trip data is unavailable, label example content as placeholder and do not present it as live inventory.

## When there are no authentic images

For a local dental clinic that requests a warm, trustworthy homepage but supplies no licensed photography, use an appointment-card motif, welcoming service explanations, readable Vietnamese type, and a clear visit sequence. The first viewport could put a plain-language headline and verified contact route on the left, with a large appointment-paper composition on the right containing only approved practical information. Reuse its paper edge or fine rule as a section divider, then shift to a quiet service list and a numbered visit guide. This can be distinctive without a fake smiling-patient hero. Keep names, qualifications, treatments, opening hours, address, prices, and clinical claims in a source/approval ledger; do not publish them until verified. A “Book a visit” control needs an actual phone, messaging, or booking destination before the page can be called live.
